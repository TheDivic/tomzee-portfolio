Lokal (weRate)

Foursquare disruptor

Mobile App, 2025-2026

Who are Lokal?

Although an app that allows the users to check in to various venues is far from a first in the modern tech space, weRate embarked on a quest to incentivize the users by rewarding them. Discover great places, check in to them, and participate in a review aggregate that helps the users shape the reputation of their favorite venues and hangout spots.

My role and responsibilities

UX design lead

I joined weRate as a lead (and sole) designer on the project. Although the app was already functioning at the point, it had a long way to realizing its true potential. At weRate, I successfully juggled accomplishing the company’s design needs, while finding time to work on the complete visual and functional re-haul of the app.

Opportunities and goals of the project

Knocking out daily design and maintenance activities, while finding time to redesign and improve the app’s experience.

Even before I was officially onboarded, the first thing I said to the CEO regarding the then-state of weRate was “weRate doesn’t know what it is”. At the state the app was when I joined, the product had the functionality of checking in to and reviewing venues, but it didn’t feel like a check-in app. There was no map, and it was riddled with features of questionable necessity. This provided a great opportunity to make the app look and feel great, while retaining the old and adding new functionalities.

Timeline illustrationsassets/img/lokal-rail-2.png

Biggest challenge faced

The leadership was tough to break when I proposed a re-haul of the app. So, I kept quiet and focused on fixing the small UX and design system issues. In fact, I completely reimagined the design system, improving it in every sense. However, I wasn’t gonna go down quietly.

Under wraps, as a sole designer of the app, I took the risk of leveraging various AI tools to reimagine and redesign the entire app - in terms of looks, branding, as well as functionality. I stripped away a lot of unnecessary reward features, and the result was exactly what I wanted to create - a check in and review app that doesn’t ask too many questions, yet provides enough feedback for the system to work.

Outcome and learnings

The greatest challenge with Lokal is also where I learned humility as a designer. Even though the management loved the work I did and despite the fact that the redesign was now officially planned and set in motion, there was some pushback in terms of the features I removed. This is where I learned that it’s not all about the UX and the functionality of a product - it’s very much about the business needs. Setting aside my ego (this was a tough one), I successfully redesigned the app, to the requirements of the leadership.
